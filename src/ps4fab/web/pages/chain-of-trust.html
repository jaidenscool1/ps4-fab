How a PS4 decides what to trust

From the moment you press power, each stage checks the next one before handing over control. This page follows that chain, shows where a jailbreak gets in, and what you can study on your own boards with the gear on the bench today.

The boot chain

Same on every consolePer-console, locked to the APUFused in silicon

Simplified. The southbridge chip has two small processors of its own, EMC and EAP. The EMC loads its first loader from the NOR, and that loader starts the EAP kernel loader and the APU's boot ROM (MediaCon, Southbridge). The secure loader is stored in the NOR as sflash0s1.cryptx2 (Codenames). The wiki is community-written and marks parts of this as incomplete.

Three layers of keys

Fused root keys

Burned into the security processor at the factory. They are used inside the chip and never written to the NOR, RAM or anywhere a probe can reach. Fuses sit inside the die and are read only by the chip's own logic.

Wrapped per-console keys

Derived from the root keys and stored encrypted in the NOR. They unlock this console's identity data and its hard drive. Only this APU can unwrap them, which is why one console's NOR won't boot on another board.

Shared firmware keys

Sign and encrypt the system software, the same on every console. This layer has been studied publicly in depth.

What a jailbreak can and can't do

Can

  • Run with full kernel control on that console
  • Ask its own security processor to unwrap its own keys
  • Make decrypted backups of its own NOR data and drive key

Can't

  • Read the fused root keys from software (on the PS5 they reportedly leaked anyway, see the table)
  • Make one console's identity work on another board
  • Run at all on a console that doesn't boot, or on firmware newer than the bug it uses

What you can learn with what's on the bench

have it

Watch the symptom

Power each console and write down what happens: light bar colour and pattern, beeps, fan spin, picture or not. That's a crude view of how far the chain got.

have it

Multimeter on the power side

With the console unplugged, check rails for shorts to ground (a shorted capacitor is a common fault) and check the power supply outputs against its label. If power never comes up, stage 1 never starts.

have it

Look closely

Phone macro photos of the HDMI port pins, the APU edge and burnt or cracked parts. Compare against the Teardown Tracer.

have it

Hard drive health on a PC

The contents are encrypted, but a PC can still read the drive's SMART health. That rules the drive in or out.

~$5 later

USB-UART adapter (CP2102)

Optional. Reads the boot log from the pads on the board bottom, so you see the chain line by line. It only listens. Plugs into the diag tool's UART tab.

~$10 later

CH341A v1.7 + SOIC16 clip

Reads a backup of each NOR. Open it in the diag tool to see which parts are per-console. Do this before any repair that risks the board.

Questions to work through together

  1. Where does each of your three consoles stop, judging by the symptom? Same place, or three different faults?
  2. How would you tell a hardware fault (a power rail, a cracked solder ball) from a firmware fault (bad NOR data, syscon mismatch)?
  3. If the root key is burned into the chip, why can't you just measure it with a probe?
  4. Why does the hard drive have its own per-console encryption, when the NOR is already protected?
  5. What would a "repair" look like at each stage: a replaced part, a rewritten chip, or nothing you can fix?

The order here is the boot order: answering 1 first makes the others easier.
